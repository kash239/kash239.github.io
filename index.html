Depreciation

Click to Decode

Definition: The systematic allocation of the cost of a tangible asset over its useful lifespan due to wear and tear.

Real-World Example: Purchasing a brand-new car; the moment you drive it off the lot, its value instantly drops.

Liquidity

Click to Decode

Definition: The relative speed and ease with which an asset can be converted directly into cash without losing its value.

Real-World Example: A checking account balance is highly liquid; a commercial building takes months to sell, making it highly illiquid.

Capital Gains

Click to Decode

Definition: The net profit earned from selling an asset for a price higher than its initial purchase cost.

Real-World Example: Buying a share of stock at $10 and selling it later to another investor when the market price hits $50.

Amortization

Click to Decode

Definition: An accounting framework used to systematically reduce the cost basis of an intangible asset or pay down loan obligations via equal monthly installments.

Real-World Example: A fixed car loan payment where each month a portion pays off the calculated interest and the rest reduces the original debt principal.

Stocks & Equities

Click to Decode

Definition: Securities representing fractional ownership units in a public corporation, entitling the holder to proportional claims on corporate residual earnings.

Real-World Example: Owning shares of Apple; you legally own a micro-percentage of the business and profit directly when product innovation drives stock appreciation.
